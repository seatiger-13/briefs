1. Lean 與 Mathlib 社群發表聯合聲明，核實 Anthropic 數學家 Levent Alpöge 在 Claude 協助下完成的黎曼猜想 Lean 證明，並表示對證明正確有充分信心

Lean 社群博客於 2026-11-21 刊登由 Lean 與 Mathlib 社群發表、並獲 Lean FRO 及 Mathlib Initiative 背書的聯合聲明。聲明指，Anthropic 數學家 Levent Alpöge 於 11 月 20 日公布一份約 1,700 萬行的 Lean 庫，作為黎曼猜想的證明；社群在過去一日核實後，認證該猜想的陳述與證明均正確，並表示「我們對 Alpöge 的黎曼猜想證明正確有充分信心，並祝賀他取得這項成果」。聲明稱已滿足四項標準：有關陳述即 Mathlib 中已由 Lean 及數論專家仔細審視的 RiemannHypothesis；多人已成功編譯該庫並得到相同的原始證明匯出檔，編譯過程沒有錯誤或建置失敗；社群加入一個只匯入 Mathlib、用以獨立列出該陳述的檔案，並以 comparator 及 con-leche --compare 兩套獨立工具比對，確認 Alpöge 庫所證明的定理與 Mathlib 的陳述完全相同；匯出的原始證明已由 Lean 官方核心、con-leche、con-ron，以及其他以不同程式語言編寫的獨立檢查器核對，其中 con-leche 與 con-ron 附有相對於集合論、指它們不會接受錯誤證明的形式化證明，而且這些檢查是在惰性匯出檔上進行，以避開證明原始碼中假想惡意程式的影響。Alpöge 在個人網站的說明中寫道，他相信自己「與 Claude 一起」發現了黎曼猜想的證明，並稱該方法很容易進一步解決廣義黎曼猜想；Lean 社群這份已核實的公開聲明所認證的，是黎曼猜想本身，本簡報並不把廣義黎曼猜想說成已獲同等認證。《科學人》報道，帝國學院倫敦的 Kevin Buzzard 早前提出，須確認 Lean 程式所證明的是否正好是該猜想、以及程式是否重新定義了陳述中的符號；該刊並引述麻省理工學院數學家 Andrew Sutherland 稱，證明已在只使用三條標準公理的情況下，由 Lean 核心編譯並獨立覆核，「誠實地說，當中有問題的機會大約為零」。《衛報》報道，Alpöge 稱 Claude 在約六週內協助發展該證明，並引述他寫道，接近終點時「Claude 遠遠拋離了我」。黎曼猜想是克萊數學研究所七道千禧年獎問題之一，該所為每道題懸賞 100 萬美元；截稿時並未核實該所已就今次證明作出獎項決定，本簡報亦不把該獎視為已經頒發。亦未見 Anthropic 公司就此事發表已核實的正式公開聲明。

Lean and Mathlib communities issue a joint statement certifying Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge’s Claude-assisted Lean proof of the Riemann hypothesis, saying they have full confidence it is correct

The Lean community blog on 2026-11-21 published a joint statement by the Lean and Mathlib communities, endorsed by the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative. It said Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge released a proof of the Riemann hypothesis on November 20 as a Lean library comprising 17 million lines of code. After a day of checking, the communities said they hereby certify that both the statement and the proof of the Riemann hypothesis are correct, and that they “have full confidence that Alpöge’s proof of the Riemann Hypothesis is correct, and we congratulate him on this achievement.” The statement said four criteria had been met: the statement is Mathlib’s RiemannHypothesis, which has been carefully vetted by experts in Lean and number theory; dozens of people had reported that the library compiles correctly, reproducing the same raw proof export file, without errors or build failures; the community added a file that isolates the statement and imports only Mathlib, and two independent tools, comparator and con-leche --compare, confirmed that the theorem proved in Alpöge’s library is exactly Mathlib’s statement; and the exported raw proof has been checked by several proof checkers, including Lean’s official kernel, the con-leche and con-ron checkers, and several independent checkers written in different programming languages. The statement said con-leche and con-ron come with a formal proof, relative to set theory, that they cannot accept a false proof, and that the checks ran on an inert export, out of reach of hypothetical malicious code in the Lean sources. In a note on his personal website, Alpöge wrote that he believed he had, “working with Claude,” discovered a proof of the Riemann hypothesis, and that the method readily extends to the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis. The verified public certification described here is of the Riemann hypothesis itself; this brief does not treat the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis as having received the same certification. Scientific American reported that Kevin Buzzard of Imperial College London had earlier stressed the need to confirm that the Lean code proves the actual theorem and does not redefine symbols in the statement, and quoted MIT mathematician Andrew Sutherland as saying the proof had been compiled and independently rechecked by the Lean kernel with only the three standard axioms, and that “the chance that anything is fishy here is approximately zero.” The Guardian reported that Alpöge said Claude had helped develop the solution over about six weeks, and quoted him as writing that, near the finish, “Claude far outpaced me.” The Riemann hypothesis is one of the Clay Mathematics Institute’s seven Millennium Prize Problems, for which the institute has offered $1 million each. No verified prize decision by the institute had been seen by the cutoff, and this brief does not treat the prize as awarded. No verified formal public statement from Anthropic as a company had been seen either.
